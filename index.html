The Problem

Your Booth Is Not Your Strategy. It's Just the Stage.

Most companies spend 90–100% of their tradeshow budget on the booth and operations — and put $0 into the sales and marketing tactics that actually drive results.

💰

The Budget Is Backwards

Every 100 sq ft of booth space is roughly a $10,000 investment when you factor in buildout, samples, travel, and staffing. A 1,000 sq ft booth isn't 10x more effective than a 100 sq ft booth. Yet most companies pour everything into size and nothing into strategy.

🚫

Zero Pre-Show Strategy

80% of exhibitors put zero thought into pre-show marketing. Marketing research shows it takes 6–7 touchpoints before someone takes action. If you wait until show day, you're starting from zero while your competitors already have meetings booked.

📄

Contacts Without Context

Everyone collects badge scans. Every attendee gives their info to dozens of companies. Without capturing the context of each conversation — their plans, their challenges — your follow-up is just generic outreach that gets deleted.

📈

No Way to Measure ROI

Leadership asks "was the show worth it?" and you give a vague answer about "brand awareness." A good strategy lets you forecast KPIs before committing and measure against them after — so you can justify the budget and increase it next year.

The Strategic Framework

The Best Exhibitors Don't Have the Biggest Booth. They Have the Best Strategy.

After advising hundreds of exhibitors, I've codified the patterns that separate top performers from the rest into a complete operating system. Here are the core frameworks inside it.

01

The 4-Question Filter

Before committing $25K+ to any show, you should answer 4 questions: Is my audience here? Are my competitors here? Can I differentiate? Does the show have the infrastructure to support me? Most exhibitors skip this and pay the price.

02

The 20/45/35 Budget Rule

The single biggest reason exhibitors waste money. Top performers allocate 20% to booth space, 45% to operational expenses, and 35% to sales & marketing tactics. Most companies put 90–100% into booth + operations and $0 into what actually drives ROI.

03

The 4-Layer Tactic Framework

There are 100+ possible pre-show and on-site tactics. The wrong order means wasted money. Build in layers: Foundation tactics first (online profile, free invites), then Digital, then Low-Effort On-Site, then Experiential. Skip a layer at your own risk.

04

The 5 Goal Categories

Not every tactic fits every exhibitor. Match tactics to your primary goal: Branding, Thought Leadership, Product Activation, Networking, or Traffic Driving. A tactic that's brilliant for one goal is wasteful for another.

05

Booth Selection Truths

90% of attendees visit all three halls at some point. Which hall matters less than most exhibitors think. What matters more: who your neighboring exhibitors are and where you're positioned within the hall. Position trumps prestige.

06

Context Over Contacts

Badge scan volume is a vanity metric. What matters is what's happening inside each prospect's business. A conversation where you captured business context is worth 10 generic badge scans. This changes how you staff and train your booth team.

07

The 48-Hour Rule

Industry standard for post-show follow-up: 10 business days. Top exhibitors do it in 48 hours, with personalized outreach referencing specific booth conversations. Every hour you wait, your lead gets colder. This is where most tradeshow ROI is lost.

08

30/60/90 ROI Reporting

ROI isn't a vague feeling about whether the show was worth it. It's a number. Forecast KPIs before you commit. Measure against them at 30, 60, and 90-day checkpoints. Report real numbers to stakeholders. That's how good exhibitors become great ones.
