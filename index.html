The Problem

Your Booth Is Not Your Strategy. It's Just the Stage.

Most companies spend 90–100% of their tradeshow budget on the booth and operations — and put $0 into the sales and marketing tactics that actually drive results.

💰

The Budget Is Backwards

Every 100 sq ft of booth space is roughly a $10,000 investment when you factor in buildout, samples, travel, and staffing. A 1,000 sq ft booth isn't 10x more effective than a 100 sq ft booth. Yet most companies pour everything into size and nothing into strategy.

🚫

Zero Pre-Show Strategy

80% of exhibitors put zero thought into pre-show marketing. Marketing research shows it takes 6–7 touchpoints before someone takes action. If you wait until show day, you're starting from zero while your competitors already have meetings booked.

📄

Contacts Without Context

Everyone collects badge scans. Every attendee gives their info to dozens of companies. Without capturing the context of each conversation — their plans, their challenges — your follow-up is just generic slop that gets deleted.

📈

No Way to Measure ROI

Leadership asks "was the show worth it?" and you give a vague answer about "brand awareness." A good strategy lets you forecast KPIs before committing and measure against them after — so you can justify the budget and increase it next year.

The Mindset Shift

The Best Exhibitors Don't Have the Biggest Booth. They Have the Best Strategy.

What Most Companies Do

  • Spend 90–100% of budget on the booth
  • Show up and hope for foot traffic
  • Collect badge scans with no context
  • Follow up with generic emails a week later
  • Can't tell leadership if it was worth it

What Top Performers Do

  • Put 25%+ of budget into sales & marketing tactics
  • Book meetings before the show opens
  • Capture business context in every conversation
  • Follow up within 48 hours with personalized outreach
  • Forecast & measure ROI with real KPIs

Some of the best-performing clients invest ~$10K in the smallest booth possible and $250K+ in sales and marketing tactics — and get better ROI than companies with massive booths and zero strategy.

The Masterclass

Tradeshow Masterclass: 8 Modules to Maximize Your ROI

The complete system — pre-show, on-site, and post-show — that turns your tradeshow investment into measurable business growth.

01

Choosing the Right Show

Start with your Ideal Customer Profile, not emotions or competitors. Pick 2–4 shows where your ICP actually attends and go deep instead of spreading thin across 20.

02

The Budget Mindset Shift

Stop spending 90–100% on the booth. The 25% rule: allocate at least 25% of your total budget to supportive sales and marketing. The booth is the stage — everything around it drives results.

03

Booth Location — What Actually Matters

90% of NRA Show attendees visit all three halls. Think about your neighbors and position within the hall — not just which hall. Location supports strategy, it doesn't replace it.

04

Pre-Show Strategy

Optimize your online profile (keywords, categories, photos, collateral). Drive traffic with featured exhibitor placement, targeted email campaigns to confirmed attendees, and retargeting ads. At the 2025 NRA Show: 147,000+ attendee searches on Show To Go.

05

On-Site Activations

VIP badge bag drops, show floor tours (~105 leads), sponsored meetups with your ICP, education sessions (~100 attendees + recording for repurposing), workshop sponsorships (~200 attendees), and VIP suites at the official after party.

06

Working the Floor — Context Over Contacts

It's not about badge scan volume. Capture what's happening in each person's business so your follow-up is personalized. "I know you're expanding 10 locations" beats "Hey, we met at the show."

07

Post-Show Follow-Up

The 48-hour rule. Personalized outreach based on booth conversations. Segment leads hot/warm/cold. Propose specific next steps — samples, demos, calls. Every hour you wait, your lead gets colder.

08

Measuring ROI & Improving Year Over Year

Forecast KPIs before you commit, measure against them after, report real numbers to stakeholders, and optimize year over year. That's how good exhibitors become great ones.
